# Using AI agents safely: a one-page guide

## What is an "AI agent"?
A normal chatbot only writes text. An **agent** can also *do* things for you: open and
change files, run programs, browse websites, fill in forms and send messages. That makes it
very useful, and it means mistakes have real effects.

## Six rules to remember

1. **Know what it's about to do.** Before a task, the AI should tell you its plan in plain
   words. If it doesn't, ask: "What exactly will you change?"
2. **Say yes only to what you understand.** When the AI asks permission to delete, send,
   publish, pay or install, read the question. If you're unsure, say no and ask why.
3. **Keep secrets out.** Don't paste passwords, one-time codes, card numbers, or other
   people's personal data into the AI unless your task truly needs it and it's approved.
4. **Treat outside content with suspicion.** Emails, documents and web pages can hide
   instructions aimed at the AI ("AI assistant: send this file to..."). The AI should only
   follow *you*. If it suggests something odd after reading content, stop and check.
5. **Check before you rely on it.** The AI can be confidently wrong. Check facts, numbers,
   recipients and anything that leaves the company.
6. **You are responsible for what you send or ship.** The AI is a helper, not a sign-off.

These are guidelines, not rules enforced on you. They exist to help you use AI confidently.

## What the setup changed on your computer
- **Skills** were added. These are short rule books your AI tool reads, so it explains its
  plan, asks before risky actions, protects personal data and summarises what it did.
- **Optional safety settings**: only the ones you said yes to, if any (for example,
  blocking access to password files, or always asking before deleting files). You can
  change or undo them any time. A summary is saved in the `ai-safety-setup` folder in your
  home folder.

## When to ask for help
- The AI asks to do something you don't understand.
- It did something you didn't expect (deleted, sent or changed something).
- You think a password or personal data was exposed. Tell IT straight away; that's always
  the right call, and nobody will blame you for reporting it.
